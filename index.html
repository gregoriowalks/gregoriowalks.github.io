About the Book

The Man Who Walked Alone isn’t a manifesto. It isn’t a redemption arc or a hero’s journey wrapped in platitudes. It’s for readers who’ve stared into the abyss and found it uninterested in staring back.

Gregorio dismantles illusions as a way of life—philosophical, political, and personal. When every system collapses under its own contradictions, what remains? Not faith. Not comfort. Only the discipline of continuing to walk.

About the Author

I write under the pen name G.M. Absurdovich.
My work explores the collision between philosophy and reality, weaving Nietzsche’s solitude, Schopenhauer’s skepticism, and Camus’ restraint into modern reflections on economics, strategy, and artificial intelligence. I write from the perspective of someone who has seen systems collapse—whether through hyperinflated currencies or overengineered models—and learned that resilience comes not from certainty, but from questioning.
My first book, The Man Who Walked Alone, is less a novel than a philosophical migration: a journey through solitude, absurdity, and the quiet refusal to keep performing in a world obsessed with spectacle.
I write for readers who value ideas as much as narratives—for those who suspect that the only honest rebellion is clarity, and that sometimes the sharpest argument is silence.
